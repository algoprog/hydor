# Writing

Clear writing comes from knowing who reads it and what they should take away, settling the structure
before the sentences, and editing in separate passes.

## 1. Brief first

Before drafting, settle these from the request, the material, or a question (for a large piece, see
the clarify-first skill):

- **Reader:** who, what they already know, what they need.
- **Purpose:** what the reader should know, decide or do after reading.
- **Form:** kind of text, length, tone, format (an email, Markdown, a Word document), deadline.
- **Material:** facts, sources and examples it must use; anything to avoid.

Write these down in three to five lines; they are the test for every later choice.

## 2. Structure before sentences

- State the main point in one or two sentences. If you cannot yet, you are not ready to draft.
- Outline the sections in the order the reader needs them. For practical writing, lead with the
  conclusion or the request, then the support. For a story or a speech, set up, build, resolve.
- Give each section one job, and a heading that says what it is.

## 3. Draft

- Write straight through, in plain words: short sentences, concrete nouns, active verbs, examples
  rather than abstractions. Match the tone that was asked for.
- Put what matters first, in the piece, in each section and in each paragraph.
- Mark gaps as `[to check: …]` rather than inventing facts, names or numbers.

## 4. Edit in passes

One concern per pass; details in `references/edit-passes.md`.

1. **Purpose:** does it do its job for this reader? Cut what does not serve it.
2. **Structure:** order, headings, links between sections; the main point visible in the first lines.
3. **Paragraphs and sentences:** one idea each; cut filler, hedges and repeats; vary the length.
4. **Words:** plain over fancy, the same term for the same thing, no jargon the reader lacks.
5. **Facts:** names, numbers, dates, quotes and links checked (see evidence-before-done).
6. **Polish:** spelling, grammar, punctuation, formatting, length.

Read the result once at speaking pace: anything you stumble on, the reader will too.

## 5. Deliver

- Give it in the form asked for. For a Word document, slides or a PDF, follow the skill for that
  kind of file.
- Add a short note: what you assumed, what the user should check, and easy variations (shorter, more
  formal, another audience).

## Editing someone else's text

Keep their voice and intent. Fix what is wrong, suggest what is weak, and say why for anything
substantial. Offer a light edit or a deeper rewrite when it is not clear which they want.
