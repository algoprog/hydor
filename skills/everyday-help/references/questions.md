# Questions for everyday help

Starting points, not a script. Ask one, listen, and follow the most useful thread in their words.
Skip the areas that do not fit their life.

## Opening

- "What would make this worth ten minutes for you: calmer mornings, fewer things forgotten, less time
  looking things up, or something else?"
- "How much time do you have: a quick look, or a proper chat?"
- "Is there anything you'd rather I didn't ask about, or that an assistant should never do?"

## The shape of the day

- "Walk me through yesterday, from when you got up to when you stopped for the day."
- "Which part of that took more effort than it should have?"
- "Where did the day go differently from what you planned?"
- "Which moments are the squeeze: mornings, getting out of the door, the end of work, evenings?"

## Chores and admin

- "Which bills, renewals, forms or appointments do you have to keep track of?"
- "What admin do you put off until it becomes urgent?"
- "What do you do every week that feels like the same steps each time?"

## Things they forget

- "What did you last forget that cost you something: money, time, an awkward moment?"
- "What do you keep in your head because there's nowhere better to put it?"
- "Is there anything you check again and again, just in case?"

## Decisions they repeat

- "What do you decide over and over: what to cook, what to watch, what to buy, which way to go?"
- "What would make that quicker: a shortlist, a default, or the facts at the right moment?"

## Things they look up

- "What do you look up most days: the weather, travel times, prices, scores, news, opening hours?"
- "When do you need it, and where are you then: at a desk, on your phone, on the move?"
- "Is there something you'd like to hear about only when it changes?"

## People and plans

- "Who do you coordinate with: family, a partner, flatmates, a club, a team?"
- "Which dates are easy to miss: birthdays, school events, deadlines?"
- "What do you get ready before a visit, a trip, a meeting or a phone call?"

## Work that repeats

- "Which part of your work do you do the same way every week or month?"
- "What do you copy from one place to another, or sum up for someone else?"

## Learning and interests

- "What would you like to learn, or keep up with?"
- "Where do you save interesting things and never come back to them?"

## How they want help

- "Would you rather I wait to be asked, or send you something at set times?"
- "When something needs you, how do you want to hear: a notification, a weekly summary, or nothing
  until you look?"
- "Should anything from today be remembered for later, or stay in this conversation?"

## Good follow-ups

- "Can you give me a recent example?"
- "What happened just before that, and just after?"
- "How often does that happen, and how much does it bother you?"
- "What have you tried so far?"
- A play-back: "So … Have I got that right?"
- A way out: "Shall we stay with this, move on to something else, or stop here?"

## The closing summary

Fill it only from the conversation. If nothing concrete came up, say so instead of filling the lines.

```markdown
What I heard
- <a need, in their words>
- <another>

What we set up
- <the one thing; where it lives; how to pause or change it> (or: nothing yet)

For another day
- <the other idea>

Still open
- <what we did not settle>
```
