# Forms and lengths

Open this when choosing a length, checking a manuscript against what its market expects, setting
out a manuscript to submit, or writing in a fixed poetic form. Ranges are typical, not rules; check
the guidelines of the agent, publisher, magazine or contest the writer is aiming at.

## Fiction

| Form | Words |
| --- | --- |
| Flash fiction | up to about 1,000 |
| Short story | about 1,000 to 7,500 |
| Novelette | 7,500 to 17,500 |
| Novella | 17,500 to 40,000 |
| Adult novel, most genres (literary, crime, thriller, book club) | 70,000 to 100,000 |
| Romance | 50,000 to 100,000 (shorter for category romance) |
| Fantasy and science fiction | 90,000 to 120,000 for a first novel |
| Young adult (readers about 12 to 18) | 50,000 to 90,000 |
| Middle grade (readers about 8 to 12) | 30,000 to 55,000 |
| Memoir, narrative nonfiction | 70,000 to 100,000 |

A first novel well outside its range is a hard sell to agents; a self-published one is freer.

## Children's books

| Form | Length | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Board book | under 100 words | babies and toddlers |
| Picture book | under 1,000 words, usually 32 pages | text only, unless the writer illustrates too |
| Early reader | a few hundred to about 2,500 words | short sentences, words children can decode |
| Chapter book (about 6 to 9) | about 5,000 to 15,000 words | short chapters, some pictures |

Children's books take two years or more to reach shops: avoid slang and fads that date.

## Scripts

| Form | Length |
| --- | --- |
| Feature film | 90 to 120 pages (a page is about a minute on screen) |
| One-hour television drama | 50 to 65 pages |
| Half-hour comedy | 25 to 35 pages (more for multi-camera format) |
| Full-length stage play | about 90 to 120 minutes |

Scripts have a strict layout (scene headings such as `INT. LIGHTHOUSE - NIGHT`, character names
above their lines, 12-point Courier). Use screenwriting software, or the plain-text Fountain format,
which such software opens.

## Poetry

| Form | Shape |
| --- | --- |
| Haiku | three short lines; in English often 5, 7 and 5 syllables, with a seasonal image and a turn |
| Sonnet | 14 lines, usually iambic pentameter; Petrarchan (8 lines, a turn, 6) or Shakespearean (three quatrains and a couplet, ABAB CDCD EFEF GG) |
| Villanelle | 19 lines: five three-line stanzas and a quatrain, two refrains, rhymes ABA |
| Sestina | six six-line stanzas and a three-line close, the same six end-words in rotating order |
| Ghazal | five or more couplets, each complete in itself, the same word or phrase ending each couplet |
| Pantoum | quatrains where lines 2 and 4 of each become lines 1 and 3 of the next |
| Free verse | no fixed metre or rhyme; the line break and the image do the work |

Poems go to literary magazines, usually three to five at a time. A collection: a chapbook is about
20 to 40 pages, a full collection 48 to 100.

## Manuscript layout for submissions

- 12-point Times New Roman (or a similar plain font), double-spaced, margins of 2.5 cm (one inch).
- Paragraphs indented about 1.25 cm (half an inch), with no blank line between them; text aligned
  left, not justified.
- A header with surname, short title and page number; each chapter on a new page.
- A scene break marked by a centred `#` or `* * *`.
- A title page with the title, the writer's name and contact details, and the word count rounded to
  the nearest thousand.
- A Word file (.docx) unless the guidelines ask for something else. Follow each agent's or
  magazine's own instructions over these defaults.

## What each genre promises its readers

- **Romance:** the love story is the centre, and the ending is happy, or at least hopeful for the couple.
- **Mystery:** fair play: the clues are on the page before the solution, and the detective solves it
  by reasoning, not luck or a last-minute confession.
- **Thriller:** danger and a clock; the stakes rise until the end.
- **Fantasy and science fiction:** the world's rules hold; the first book of a series still ends.
- **Horror:** dread that builds; what is unseen is often stronger than what is shown.
- **Children's:** the young hero solves their own problem.
