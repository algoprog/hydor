# Fourteen styles

Choose the style after the structure, for the reader, the subject and the medium; any style fits any
trade. For a real choice, show two or three on the same content so the difference is the style
alone. Each prompt line goes into `generate_image` prompts for that style's pictures, together with
the subject, the composition, the palette and "no text" (the image-prompts skill). No style is a
reason to invent a fact or borrow someone's branding.

**Editorial.** Suits evidence and comparisons the reader should grasp quickly. A dominant headline,
aligned columns, thin rules, wide margins; a serif headline with a plain sans for the rest; dark grey
on a light neutral with one accent; charts labelled directly. Not for a mechanism that needs one big
central picture. Prompt line: "a restrained editorial illustration, flat muted colours, generous
empty space".

**Technical.** Suits parts, mechanisms and how things fit together. Even line weights, clear outlines
of each part, numbered callouts, labelled connectors; a cutaway or exploded view only when it shows a
real relationship. Never invent dimensions; poor fit when the mechanism is not known. Prompt line: "a
clean technical illustration with consistent line weights, a simplified cutaway, neutral background".

**Pictogram.** Suits quick actions, wayfinding and compact guides. Icons drawn alike with a plain verb
under each, one action per block, numbers only where order matters. Explain unusual symbols; when
icons count things, say what one icon stands for and draw fractions honestly. Prompt line: "a set of
simple pictograms, one consistent style, two colours, rounded strokes".

**Playful flat.** Suits friendly explanations for a general audience. Simple shapes, a small bright
palette, people of many kinds, dark labels on calm areas. Not for sad or serious subjects. Prompt
line: "a friendly flat vector scene, simple geometric shapes, bright limited palette, calm areas
left plain".

**Paper cut.** Suits tactile stories, cycles and gentle comparisons. Layered paper shapes with
shallow, even shadows; broad silhouettes; words on flat, untextured areas. Not for dense charts or
small labels; paper thickness must never stand for amounts. Prompt line: "a layered cut-paper
scene, broad simple shapes, soft shallow shadows, flat plain areas".

**Field guide.** Suits identifying things and comparing forms (plants, birds, tools, parts). Careful
drawings of each item from the same angle, callouts on the features that tell them apart, detail
insets, ink and wash. Only features you have checked; mark different scales; never where a wrong
identification could harm (wild mushrooms). Prompt line: "an observational field-guide plate, ink
and watercolour, each item from the same angle, white background".

**Comic.** Suits a change, a decision or an experience told in order. Numbered panels, one change
per panel, the same characters throughout, few words. Give a panel-by-panel text version; invented
dialogue is marked as an example, and never put in the mouth of a real person. Prompt line: "a
comic panel, clean line art, flat colours, the same character as before, simple background".

**Photo annotation.** Suits explaining what can be seen in a real photograph. One large photo, callouts
anchored to points on it, detail crops, labels on solid backgrounds. Keep the evidence visible; a
generated scene must never stand in for a photo of a real event. Prompt line: none: use the person's
own photo.

**Geometric poster.** Suits one strong message with little else. Bold shapes, a big short headline,
two or three ink-like colours, much empty space. The qualifying line stays readable and the headline
no stronger than the evidence; no long text in capitals. Prompt line: "a bold geometric poster
background, two inks on off-white, large empty area for a headline".

**Isometric.** Suits showing where things are in a system or a place. One consistent angled view,
simple parts, clear zones and paths, one light source. Put exact numbers in flat charts beside it:
apparent volume must not stand for amounts. Prompt line: "a simplified isometric illustration,
consistent projection, clean flat shapes, nothing hidden behind anything".

**Notebook.** Suits informal explanations, study notes and summaries. Tidy hand-drawn outlines and
highlighter marks, typeset body text, a paper tone, a clear route through the page. No handwritten
body text and no scribbles over data. Prompt line: "tidy notebook sketches, controlled pen lines, a
few highlighter accents, cream paper".

**Learning diagram.** Suits introducing a concept to beginners. One central idea, explained in steps,
with a worked example; large plain type and consistent colours for each idea. Simplify without
teaching a false rule; respect adult beginners (no childish decoration). Prompt line: "a clear
educational illustration of one concept, simple shapes, consistent colour per idea".

**Atlas.** Suits places, routes and patterns across an area. Real geography on a quiet base map, the
theme laid over it, direct labels, a nearby key, scale and north where they matter, the date and
source. Never invent geography; maps of rates rather than counts (the dashboards skill). Prompt
line: none for the map itself: draw it from real data (the maps-and-places skill).

**Sculpted.** Suits a memorable metaphor made of objects. Matte clay or moulded shapes, soft even
light, broad silhouettes, labels on flat planes beside them. Describe the metaphor in the text
version; object size never stands for amounts; not for solemn subjects. Prompt line: "a matte clay
sculpture scene, soft studio light, simple rounded forms, plain background".
