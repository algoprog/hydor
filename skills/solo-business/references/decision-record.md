# The decision record

One page, kept where they can find it. Unknowns stay marked as unknown. Make a new version whenever
the offer, the price or the hours change, and work the numbers out again.

```markdown
# <Business>: <the decision> (<date>)

**Decision.** <go on / change / stop / test first>: <one or two sentences>.

**What they need from it.** <income> a month by <date>; <hours> hours a week; can risk <amount>;
can manage <months> months without income from it. Aim: <side income / replace a salary / …>.

**The offer.** For <who>, <what> so that <result>, for <price>, within <time>.
Not included: <…>. Replies: <when, how fast>.

**Evidence so far.** <date, source, what happened: paid / committed / said they liked it>.
Against it: <…>. Still unknown: <…>.

**Numbers.** Price <…>; that sale's own costs <…>; each sale brings in <…>. Fixed costs <…> a week.
Hours: <total> − selling <…> − admin <…> − support and reserve <…> = <…> delivery hours;
<…> hours a sale, so <…> sales fit. Sales needed for the income goal: <…>. Fits: yes / no.

**The main bottleneck.** <buyers / hours / price / support / cash>.

**Next test.** <what>, at most <hours> and <money>, until <date>.
Go on if <…>; change if <…>; stop if <…>.

**Look again on** <date>.
```

## Worked examples (made up)

**The price floor.** They need €2,500 a month for themselves, have €300 a month of business costs, and
an accountant suggests setting aside €800 a month for tax: €3,600 a month. They can deliver 10 hours a
week; with six weeks off a year for holidays and illness, that is 10 × 46 ÷ 12 ≈ 38.3 hours a month.
Floor: 3,600 ÷ 38.3 ≈ €94 an hour. A quote for a 5-hour job under €470 pays less than they need.

**Does it fit the week?** Twelve hours a week: 3 for selling, 1 for admin, 2 for support and reserve,
6 for delivery. A fixed-scope report takes 3 hours including checking, so 2 a week fit. Price €400,
own costs €40: each brings in €360. Fixed costs €100 a week, and they want €1,000 a week for
themselves: 1,100 ÷ 360 = 3.06, so 4 reports, twice what fits. Decision: do not leave the job on this
plan. Options: €600 a report (€560 each, 2 reports give €1,120: enough); a 2-hour report (3 fit, €1,080:
still just short); a lower goal; or more hours. A pilot can still test demand, with a cap on what it may
lose, but it does not prove the income goal.

**A sale that loses money.** A subscription at €30 a month costs €8 in hosting and €28 of their own
support time, valued at the rate they set for their hours: −€6 a customer. At 100 customers that is
−€600 a month before fixed costs; tripling them makes it −€1,800. Test what cuts support (clearer
onboarding, a narrower scope, a higher price), measuring real support time, or stop the offer.

**Interest is not demand.** 500 people on a waiting list and 12 compliments for a paid newsletter; they
can spend €300 and 15 hours testing. Do not build a platform. Write one paid issue by hand and offer it
to the list: go on if at least the number they chose beforehand (say 25) pay by the end of the month;
stop or change the idea otherwise.

**More work at the old price.** A €600-a-month service took 4 hours and €50 of tools: about €137 an
hour. The client now wants daily custom reports, 10 more hours a month: 14 in all, which brings it to
about €39 an hour. Offer the standard weekly report at the old price, or quote for the daily one.

**Automate, pay someone, or keep doing it?** A task takes 4 hours a week. Automating it takes 10 hours
to set up, then 1.5 hours a week of checking and half an hour of fixing: it saves 2 hours a week, so the
setup pays back after 5 weeks, before the tool's cost and only if the quality holds (which is an
assumption until it has been checked on real work). A contractor saves delivery time but needs
briefing, checking and a plan for when they are away. Compare over the months it will run.

**One big client.** One client brings 70% of the income. Losing them would leave rent unpaid within two
months. Next test: two new clients from referrals within eight weeks; meanwhile keep a month of costs in
reserve, and do not take on fixed costs on the strength of that one client.

**Famous success stories.** A founder who sold a company for millions, usually with a team behind them,
says nothing about this business's income. Plan from their own numbers and their own customers.
