# Testing thumbnails and reading the numbers

As YouTube's help pages described them in November 2026 (Test & Compare:
https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16391400). Features and rules change; check before relying
on a detail.

## Test & Compare

- **Who and where:** YouTube Studio on a computer, with the channel's advanced features turned on.
- **Which videos:** long videos and live archives. Not Shorts, scheduled live streams, Premieres
  until they have ended and become ordinary videos, or videos made for kids, for mature audiences,
  or private.
- **What:** up to three thumbnails, three titles, or three title-and-thumbnail pairs. A test of pairs
  compares the pairs: it cannot say which half made the difference.
- **How it decides:** by watch-time share, not click-through rate. The results: a winner (clearly
  ahead on watch-time share), "performed the same", or "inconclusive" (no strong difference).
- **How long:** it should finish within two weeks. Videos with few impressions end "inconclusive"
  more often.
- **Hands off:** changing the title or thumbnail during the test stops it.
- **Size:** if any thumbnail is under 1280×720, all of them are shown at 854×480.

## Plan a test

1. One question: the thumbnail, the title, or the pair.
2. Variants that differ in one clear idea each, every one true to the video.
3. Write down what you expect before it starts: "Showing the finished pantry will make the payoff
   clearer to new viewers than the flooded lot."
4. Start it, leave it alone, and read the result YouTube gives.

## Read the result

- **Winner:** use it, and note which idea won, for the next video.
- **Performed the same, or inconclusive:** there is no winner. Keep the most accurate one, or the one
  the person prefers, and say plainly that the test did not decide. Never pick by a slightly higher
  click-through rate, and never promise that a result will "scale" or bring views.

## Reading the channel's numbers

- **Impressions click-through rate:** how often people watched after YouTube showed the thumbnail on
  its own pages; impressions on other websites and in end screens are not counted. Half of all
  channels and videos sit between 2% and 10%. Small ups and downs are normal; judge after many
  impressions, not on the first day.
- **Compare like with like:** the same traffic source (Home, Suggested, Search, Subscriptions, the
  channel page) and similar periods. Rates from the channel page run higher, and a change in who is
  shown the video changes the rate without any change in the design.
- **Watch time and average view duration:** did the people who clicked stay?
- **The intro:** YouTube Studio's key moments for audience retention show how many viewers are
  still watching at 30 seconds. A high share suggests the opening matched what the title and
  thumbnail promised; a drop after a new thumbnail suggests it did not.
- **More clicks, less watching:** the pair over-promises or draws the wrong viewers. Fix the pair or
  the opening of the video.
- **No test:** a new thumbnail on an old video, compared before and after, is an observation. Say
  "after the change", not "because of it".
