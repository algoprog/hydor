# Tasks people forget, by kind of project

Go through the list for the project's kind and add what applies. The long waits are marked
(wait): start those first.

## An event (a conference, a party, a wedding, a fair)

- Date checked against holidays, school terms and other events the guests go to.
- Venue: visit, capacity, access for wheelchairs, toilets, parking, curfew, deposit and cancellation
  terms. (wait: popular venues book months ahead)
- Licences and permits: alcohol, music, outdoor space, road closure, food hygiene. (wait)
- Insurance: public liability, cancellation.
- Guest list; save-the-date, invitation, reminder a week before; RSVP deadline.
- Dietary needs and allergies collected with the RSVP; final numbers to the caterer by their date.
- Suppliers: catering, drinks, sound and screens, furniture, decoration, photography, transport;
  each with arrival time, contact on the day, and payment dates.
- Run of the day, minute by minute, with who is where; a rehearsal or tech check.
- Signs, name badges, a printed contact list, a first-aid kit, a plan for rain or heat.
- Roles on the day: a host, someone for suppliers, someone for problems.
- After: payments settled, thank-yous, photos shared, feedback asked, deposits back.

## A house move

- Notice to the landlord, or completion date agreed with the buyer and seller. (wait)
- Removal quotes (three), booking, insurance for the goods, a parking permit for the van. (wait)
- Declutter: sell, donate, recycle; book a collection for bulky items. (wait: councils and charities
  book up)
- Packing materials; a "first night" box (kettle, chargers, bedding, medicines, documents).
- Broadband at the new address. (wait: installation can take weeks)
- Meter readings on both ends on the day; gas, electricity and water switched to the new name.
- Change of address: bank, employer, doctor, dentist, insurance, tax office, vehicle registration,
  voting register, subscriptions, deliveries; mail redirection for a few months.
- Schools or nurseries, if any. (wait)
- Who looks after children and pets on the day.
- Cleaning the old place, keys handed back, deposit claimed.
- At the new place: photos of its condition, smoke alarms tested, locks changed if bought.

## A product launch

- The message: who it is for, the one line, the proof, the price.
- Launch date checked against holidays, competitors' events, and the team's leave.
- Product ready: a go/no-go meeting with clear criteria, a way to roll back.
- Website or landing page, sign-up or checkout tested end to end; tracking of visits and sign-ups.
- Help pages, FAQ, support trained, answers ready for the likely questions.
- Legal review: claims, terms, privacy, pricing pages. (wait)
- Press list, embargo dates, press kit with pictures; partners told in advance. (wait)
- Posts and emails written, approved and scheduled (the social-media and email skills).
- Sales and partners briefed; a demo that works offline.
- On the day: someone watching the site and support; a channel for problems.
- After: a review at one and four weeks: numbers against goals, what to fix.

## A renovation

- Measurements and a survey; photos of the state before.
- Permissions: planning permission, building rules sign-off, landlord's, freeholder's or
  neighbours' consent (party walls). (wait)
- Design and drawings; choices of materials made before work starts.
- Three quotes, references checked, a contract with stages, a payment schedule tied to finished
  stages, and a 10 to 20% contingency in the budget.
- Long-lead items ordered early: windows, kitchens, worktops, bespoke joinery. (wait: often 4 to 10
  weeks)
- The usual order: strip out, first fix (plumbing, wiring), plaster, second fix, fit, decorate.
- Skip or waste removal; protection for floors and other rooms; dust.
- Where to live, cook and wash meanwhile.
- Inspections and certificates (electrics, gas); warranties and manuals kept.
- A snagging list walked through before the final payment.

## Software

- Requirements written as what users can do, with acceptance criteria.
- Work cut into thin slices that each work end to end (one more thing a user can do), rather than
  layers (all the database, then all the screens): each slice shows real progress and brings
  problems out early.
- Design reviewed before building; risky parts tried out first.
- Environments: development, test, production; access for the people who need it.
- Data: migration written and rehearsed on a copy; a backup before the real run.
- Tests: automated, and acceptance testing by real users.
- Security and privacy review; secrets out of the code. (wait, where another team does it)
- Performance under expected load.
- Documentation for users and for whoever runs it.
- Release plan: feature flags or a staged rollout, monitoring and alerts, a rollback plan, and for
  each stage the signal that means stop and roll back.
- Support told what is coming; release notes; training if needed.
- After: watch errors and usage for a week; a short review of what to do differently.
