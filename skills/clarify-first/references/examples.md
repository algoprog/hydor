# Examples

How the first question, the playback and the approaches might look for different requests. These show
the shape, not a script: every question should come from what this person said and what you found.

## An app: "Build me an app to track my running"

- **Read first:** is there a folder, an existing app, a phone platform mentioned, a watch or service
  they already use?
- **First question:** "Do you want this mostly to log runs as you go, or to see how you are improving
  over months? The first is a quick entry screen; the second is mostly charts and history, and changes
  what I build first."
- **Approaches:** (1) a small web app that works on the phone's browser, quickest to build and change;
  (2) a native phone app, better offline and with notifications, much more work; (3) a spreadsheet
  with a simple form, ready today but limited. Recommend by what they said about phone and offline use.

## A trip: "Plan two weeks in Japan for us"

- **Read first:** dates, who "us" is, any bookings or wishes mentioned earlier.
- **First question:** "Is this more about cities and food, or nature and quiet places? It decides
  whether we base ourselves in two or three cities or move every few days through the countryside."
- **Playback marks:** "two adults (my assumption), mid-range hotels (my assumption), April (you said)".
- **Approaches:** a classic Tokyo, Kyoto, Osaka route with day trips; a slower route with fewer bases
  and more nature; a mix with one rural stay. Say what each costs in travel days.

## A talk: "Help me prepare a talk on our results"

- **Read first:** the results themselves (slides, a paper, a report), the event, the time slot.
- **First question:** "Who will be in the room, and what should they do or believe afterwards? A
  funding panel needs the ask up front; fellow researchers want the method and the surprises."
- **Approaches:** a story built around one surprising finding; a classic problem, method, results,
  next steps; a short talk with a demo. Recommend by audience and length.

## A renovation: "We want to redo the kitchen"

- **Read first:** photos, measurements or plans they shared, the budget if mentioned.
- **First question:** "What bothers you most about the kitchen today? Not enough storage, too little
  light and space, and simply dated looks lead to quite different plans and budgets."
- **My calls (marked):** keep the plumbing where it is (cheaper, my assumption); standard cabinet
  sizes (my assumption).
- **Approaches:** a refresh (fronts, worktop, lighting); a new layout within the same room; opening
  into the next room. Give rough cost ranges and disruption time for each, and say they are rough.
