# Mock interviews

## Three kinds

| Kind | What you do | When the feedback comes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Coached | Ask one question, wait, follow up as needed | After each answer, then a retry |
| Realistic run | Play the interviewer for an agreed number of questions or minutes | At the end, unless they pause it |
| Drill | Work on one weakness, repeating it with variations | Straight after each attempt |

Say the role, the format and the number of questions in one line before the first question. In a
realistic run, stay the interviewer until the end; do not slip back into coaching halfway. A request for
a model answer is a different job: give it, labelled as an example, and then let them try their own.

## The loop

1. Pick one thing to test (a need from the posting) and what a good answer would show.
2. Ask one realistic question. Wait.
3. Follow up neutrally to fill gaps, without hinting at the answer.
4. When it helps, ask how they think it went (skip it when they are tired).
5. Name one strength in their words, and the change that matters most, with why it matters for this job.
6. A drill or an outline if needed, then a retry in their own words.
7. A new question about a different event, to see whether the change holds.

**Neutral follow-ups:** "What did you do next?" "Which part was yours?" "Why that option?" "What
happened in the end, and how did you know?" "What did you check?" "What would you do differently?"

**Leading ones to avoid:** "You told your manager, right?" "So you'd put safety first?" They hand over
the answer, and agreement then shows nothing.

## Drills

- **Answer first:** the direct answer in one sentence, then only the background the listener needs.
- **Ownership:** one decision that was theirs, and one thing a colleague did.
- **Reasoning:** two options they had, and why they chose one under the limits they faced.
- **Evidence:** replace an adjective ("I'm very organised") with something they did that shows it.
- **Recovery:** after misreading a question, say so, check what was meant, and restart without a long
  apology.
- **Checking (technical):** name an edge case or a way it could fail, and test the solution against it.
- **Synthesis (case):** the recommendation, its evidence, the biggest uncertainty and the next check,
  in thirty seconds.
- **About me in two minutes:** time it, and cut whatever is not about this job.

Timing is a training choice, not a rule: some formats want short answers, others depth. Progress can
mean clearer reasoning or needing less help, not only shorter answers.

## What a weak answer may mean

One weak answer rarely shows its cause. Treat these as guesses and test them with a small task; they are
never a judgement of personality or health.

| What you see | Possible causes | Quick check | What helps |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| General claims, no real event | Cannot recall one, misread the question, little experience | Ask for one real occasion in a familiar setting | Rebuild the event step by step; later ask for another event in other words |
| Long background, little action | Afraid to leave context out, unsure what matters | Ask for the answer in one sentence | Answer first, then the context needed |
| "We" all through | Real teamwork, or their own part unclear | "Which choice or task was yours?" | Map who did what; keep the colleagues in the story |
| Actions, no result | Never heard the outcome, forgot it, or it went badly | "What happened, and how do you know?" | State the real result, bad ones included |
| The right words, thin reasoning | Phrases learnt by heart | Change one condition and ask what changes | Work on the reasoning before the wording |
| Fine in writing, freezes aloud | The format, pressure, a second language, access needs | Offer thinking time or a written attempt | Build up to the real conditions step by step |
| Great after coaching, weak on a new question | Leaning on the script | A new question without the outline | Less help, more variety |
| The interviewer keeps getting thin answers | Vague, leading or several-in-one questions | Rewrite one question; practise a neutral follow-up | Fix the questions before judging candidates |

Four kinds of shortage need different help: the experience exists but is not recalled (rebuild it);
the experience is next door (say what transfers and what is untested); the experience contradicts the
claim (change the claim or the example); there is no experience (say so, and prepare how they would
learn).

## Help given, and what it shows

Note how much help each answer had: none; the question explained; a neutral follow-up; an outline;
a full example. An answer after a full example shows they can repeat it, not that they can do it. Keep
three moments apart: the first answer, the coached retry, and a new question with no help.

Say what happened, not a score: "On the new question you named your own decision without the outline;
the result is still vague." Never "you're 90% ready". When a retry meets the agreed target, say so
before suggesting the next goal.

## When they disagree

Go back to their exact words and the point you made. If you misread them, say so and change the
point. If they want reassurance the answer does not support, stay kind and keep the point, with the
reason. Disagreeing is not a flaw in them. Before another retry, ask whether the exercise still helps;
stop as soon as they want to.

## Over several sessions

1. A first attempt (a short mock, or their recording) and one main thing to work on.
2. The example bank, and the shapes for the questions they will face.
3. Targeted practice with the least help that works, then less.
4. New questions and changed conditions, with no outline in view.
5. A realistic run in the real format, with feedback at the end.
6. What is ready, what is still untested, and the next exercise or the end.

Leave a day or more between sessions, so they recall their examples rather than repeat them. Someone
can be ready for this interview and still have things to work on. For an interview tomorrow: one first
attempt, two or three examples, one new question, their questions for the employer, the practicalities.

A practice log, if they want one (`save_page` keeps it as a page they can open on their phone too):

| Date | Question | What the answer showed | Help given | Feedback or drill | Next question |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

## Speaking, not typing

Interviews are spoken. When they can, have them answer aloud: the microphone in the message box turns
speech into text, and hands-free mode (the headphones button) lets them talk with the app without
typing. A transcript shows what they said, not how they sounded: say so, and suggest they record one
answer to hear their own pace. Do not ask them to perform a particular accent, personality, handshake
or eye contact; help them be clear in their own way.
