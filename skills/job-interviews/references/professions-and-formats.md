# Fields, formats and levels

Use the posting, the invitation and the employer's own instructions first; these tables only fill gaps.
A coach can judge whether someone explains a decision clearly and owns their part. Whether a clinical,
legal, safety or technical answer is correct needs the field's current standards and, where it matters,
a qualified person: say what you could not check rather than inventing an answer key.

## Formats

| Format | How to prepare |
| --- | --- |
| Recruiter or phone screen | A short "about me", why this job, the essentials (place, hours, start date, salary range), two questions |
| Competency or behavioural | Real events for each competency in the posting; STAR as a checklist |
| Situational ("what would you do") | What they would find out, what matters most, the action within their role, what if it goes wrong |
| Technical or troubleshooting | Clarify, plan, work it through, check it, say its limits; ask which tools are allowed |
| Case | Clarify the decision, a structure for this case, careful sums with units, a recommendation |
| Portfolio or a deep dive into past work | Two pieces showing different strengths: the brief, their part, the choices, the outcome |
| Practical test, audition or demonstration | Rehearse a typical task in similar conditions; a conversation cannot judge hands-on or safe technique |
| Presentation, or a lesson | Who it is for, one goal, the evidence, how they check understanding; time it, and rehearse questions out of order |
| Panel | Answer the person who asked and include the others; expect different angles |
| Group exercise | Listen, build on others, help the group move on: contribution counts, not volume |
| Recorded video (nobody live) | Ask about preparation time, retakes and time limits; practise to a camera; check light and sound |
| Assessment day, or stations (MMI) | Short separate scenarios; reset between them |
| Leadership or executive | The mandate, decisions and trade-offs, governance, results; ask about the mandate they are offered |

Before a take-home task, check what help and tools are allowed (AI included) and what they must do
themselves. Help them prepare openly; never do a restricted test for them.

## Levels

| Level | What to show | A typical question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First job or trainee | Reliability, learning, knowing when to ask | "Tell me about learning something unfamiliar. What did you do when you got stuck?" |
| Apprentice or supervised | Working safely within limits, using feedback, following procedure and knowing why | "What would make you stop and ask your supervisor?" |
| Independent | Finishing work, checking quality, handling the usual exceptions | "How did you know the work was ready to hand over?" |
| Senior specialist | Judgement on hard problems, options weighed, influence without authority, mentoring | "Which trade-off did others disagree with, and how did you decide?" |
| Team lead or manager | Delegating, workload, feedback, developing people, the team's results | "What did you delegate, and how did you check without taking it back?" |
| Director | Priorities across teams, resources, how the work is organised | "Which team goal did you change for the wider result?" |
| Executive | Direction, governance, risk, decisions on people and money, outside stakeholders | "What did you take to the board, and what did you decide yourself?" |

- **Career changers:** what carries over from real tasks, and what they still have to learn; a personal
  project is not years in the trade.
- **Returners:** readiness now first; the gap in a sentence, with no private reasons demanded.
- **Internal candidates:** the new job's needs, not their reputation in the old one.
- **Senior specialists without a team:** depth and influence; do not push them into a manager's story.
- **Executives:** test what they decided and what came from the market or the team; scale alone proves
  little.

## Fields

Practice questions are made up. Weak answers in these fields share a shape: a slogan or a big claim
("I always put patients first", "I drove transformation") where a specific action, its limits and
how it was checked should be.

| Field | Interviewers look for | A practice question | Watch out |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Software, infrastructure, security | Clarifying the problem, a sound approach, working code, tests and edge cases, trade-offs (memory, speed, cost) | "Return the first customer ID that appears twice in a list. What if none repeats?" | Run or trace code before calling it right; a fluent explanation is not working code |
| Data and analytics | The decision behind the question, data quality, a baseline, leakage, uncertainty, cause versus correlation | "A metric rose after a launch. What would you check before saying the launch caused it?" | A before-and-after comparison does not prove a cause |
| Engineering and laboratory | Requirements, assumptions, tests, tolerances, safety, failure analysis, who approves | "A test result contradicted your model. What did you check, and what changed?" | Never improvise hazardous procedures; ask which standard applies |
| Design, product, writing, creative | The brief, their own contribution, options rejected, feedback, iteration, outcome | "Show a project where feedback changed the direction. What did you keep?" | Team work claimed as one's own; impact nobody measured; a cancelled project is not a failure |
| Academic and research | Contribution, methods, alternative explanations, limits, next steps, teaching where relevant | "What is the strongest limitation of your result, and what would you test next?" | Plans in a proposal presented as results |
| Healthcare and care | Communication, noticing a concern, priorities within their role, handover, escalation, dignity, reflection | "A handover left unclear who was following up a concern. What did you do?" | No patient details; clinical correctness needs current local standards; stations (MMI) only where the process uses them |
| Teaching and training | A learning goal, checking understanding, adapting, reflection; safeguarding by local rules | "How would you know the class understood, and what would you change if not?" | "They enjoyed it" is not evidence of learning; get the lesson brief before rehearsing |
| Legal and compliance | Analysis with incomplete facts, checking sources, clear advice, confidentiality, supervision | "Tell me about a recommendation you changed when new information came in." | No privileged details; the rules depend on the jurisdiction |
| Trades, maintenance, manufacturing | Methodical checks, workmanship, safe limits, records, handover | "A fault came back after a repair was signed off. What would you check, and whom would you tell?" | Speed is not the measure when safety or quality suffers; apprentices are not judged on independent repairs |
| Retail, hospitality, customer service | Listening, accuracy, priorities with a queue, limits of authority, following through | "A customer is upset about a mistake while others wait. What do you do first?" | "The customer is always right" when safety or policy says otherwise; a cheerful script is not the test |
| Logistics, operations, administration | Accuracy, dependencies, priorities, exceptions, clear updates, records | "Two people each say their request is urgent and you cannot do both in time. What do you do?" | "I'd just work harder" is not a plan |
| Consulting and business cases | Clarifying the decision, a structure that fits, sums with units, a recommendation with its risks | "A café's sales rose from 100 to 120 while its costs rose from 80 to 110. What do you conclude?" (Profit fell from 20 to 10.) | A memorised framework; check your own arithmetic before giving feedback |
| Finance, accounting, audit, risk | Assumptions, reconciliation, controls, checking inputs, explaining uncertainty | "One forecast assumption no longer holds. How do you explain the change to the person deciding?" | Confident jargon is not competence; use made-up figures in practice |
| Sales, account management, fundraising | Finding the need before pitching, honest positioning, objections, follow-up, learning from lost deals | "A prospect will buy if you promise a feature that is not planned. What do you say?" | Big revenue figures without the territory, period and team help behind them |
| People management and leadership | Delegation, feedback, developing people, trade-offs between goals, accountability | "Describe a decision that helped one goal and made another harder. How did you review it?" | A department story inflated into company-wide authority |
| Public service and charities | The advertised behaviours or framework at the right grade, sound decisions, service, stewardship | "How did you make a recommendation when stakeholders disagreed and the evidence was incomplete?" | Agreeing with the mission is not proof of doing the work; use the framework the advert names |

For practice in any field: a first attempt without hints, feedback, then the same skill on a changed
case (a new constraint, another audience, a different fault). If the person wants an explanation or a
plan rather than a mock, give that.

## A field not listed

1. From the posting and the invitation, list the real tasks, decisions, people and limits; separate what
   is needed on day one from what will be taught.
2. Match each task to the evidence it needs. Diagnosing: observations, checks, how the fix was
   confirmed. Making or performing: choices, quality, iteration (a portfolio, an audition). Caring,
   teaching or advising: understanding needs, communication, limits. Coordinating: dependencies,
   accuracy, handover. Analysing: assumptions, sources, sums, a recommendation. Leading: authority,
   trade-offs, enabling others.
3. Write one realistic question per key task, with enough context to answer, and two neutral
   follow-ups. Call the criteria your best guess until the employer or a professional source confirms
   them; look for one with the research skill.
4. A starter that fits most jobs: "Describe a time a result did not meet the standard expected. How did
   you notice, what did you do within your role, and how did you check what happened next?"
5. If your feedback could be pasted unchanged into any other job, get a real task or example from them
   before going on.
