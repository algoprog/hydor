# 26 November 2026, kit 0.6.1

**What this record is.** It compared two ways of building the same small web app from the same plain description: with a framework (a set of commands and parts for accounts and lists, called "the kit" below) and from scratch in any stack the builder chose, by the same kind of agent, judged by one judge that knows nothing of how an app was built. From scratch passed as many lines every time and cost between 31% and 52% of the kit in every pair, so the kit has since been dropped because of this result. Its "kit" rows and every figure below are kept as the record, unchanged: the columns about the kit's commands and packages, the arm names and the group named for "the kit's own kind of thing" are as the script wrote them then, and the harness (`scripts/webapp-bench/`) no longer makes them. It now compares runs of the same description under labels of your own (a date, a model, a version of Hydor's guide). The apps these runs made are not kept in this repository.

Store made from the repository at the commit that set the core and the command to 0.6.1. Builders: Claude Code with Sonnet 5.5, each a fresh session with no memory of earlier runs, started in an empty folder with the instruction, the brief, the maker's answers and the acceptance page and nothing else; no web tool was called and nothing outside the working folder and the brief was named (read from each transcript). Six of the fourteen descriptions were run, one build each way: two of each group. For the two larger ones the change request was also made to each app by a fresh builder.

**Cost is worked out, not billed.** The builders ran on a subscription. Each run's cost is its tokens at 2 US dollars per million input (new and written to the cache), 10 per million output and 0.20 per million read from the cache. Time is the builder's working time from its first message to its report; a second turn, where the driver allowed a browser request and the builder tried its app in a browser, is included for the runs that had one (01 both arms, 18 from scratch).

**The judge was corrected three times by these runs, before any result was counted**, each time because it required something the maker's words do not: an email field nobody asked for (01, against from scratch), the maker's own phrase for a field not accepted (07, against the kit), and a field no builder was told to declare (17, against both). Every app was judged again after each correction. The self-test of the judge (reference apps of both kinds and their spoiled copies) was run by its author after each, not by the driver.

**What it shows.** All twelve apps, and all four changed apps, passed every line. From scratch cost between 31% and 52% of the kit to build in every pair, and 42% and 62% of the kit for the two changes. The difference did not narrow for the two larger descriptions. The kit-built apps changed fewer lines for the same change (71 against 118, 29 against 79) and still cost more: what the kit adds for a builder is what it has to read and run (the app's notes, the checks, a command for each step), not what it has to write.

**What this can and cannot show.** Fourteen pairs with one run each (six, four and four in the three groups) is a small sample, and four pairs in one group is smaller still. A builder agent is not deterministic: the same brief built twice by the same agent can differ by more than the
difference between the arms, and one run of each says nothing of that spread. The intervals above come from drawing the briefs again (the briefs, not the runs, are what is resampled), so they
do not include the builder's own run-to-run noise either; they are wider than they look from the median alone, and with six or four pairs a bootstrap interval is a rough guide, not a test.
The three groups are chosen, not drawn: six small descriptions where the kit is made to help, four small ones where it has no obvious head start, and four larger ones that each need several things a framework brings. A median over a group says what happens for descriptions like these, not for descriptions in general.
What it can show: a large, one-sided difference that holds in nearly every pair (the kit arm passing more lines in nearly every brief, or costing a third as much), and **where** the kit does not
help: a brief where it passed fewer lines or cost more, the lines each arm fails, and how the answer differs for the briefs that are the kit's own kind of thing and for the four that are not.
What it cannot show: that one arm is better in general (other models, other briefs, other makers), that the difference is the kit and not the notes and commands around it, how an app looks or
feels, what happens in a browser with scripts, security beyond who may see which rows and where the secrets are, or what a year of changes costs. Cost and time are the driver's, and the two
arms differ in what they are free to choose: the scratch arm picks its own stack (and may spend its time on choosing), the kit arm has one. Each arm wrote `judge.json`; that work is in both.
